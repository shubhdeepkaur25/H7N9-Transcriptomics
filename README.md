# H7N9-Transcriptomics
 Strain-Resolved Time-Course Transcriptomic Analysis of H7N9 Influenza

This study performed comparative transcriptomic profiling of human bronchial epithelial cells (Calu-3) infected with four influenza virus strains — avian H7N9, seasonal H3N2, H7N7, and highly pathogenic H5N1 (VN1203) — across multiple infection stages (3, 7, 12, and 24 hours post-infection) to characterize virus-specific host-response dynamics. The dataset comprised 40 RNA-seq samples generated from biological replicates spanning all virus–timepoint combinations, with normalized whole-genome expression data processed through log₂ transformation and stringent quality-control filtering to ensure median-centered, outlier-free distributions. Sample reproducibility and data integrity were evaluated using expression-distribution boxplots and correlation analyses demonstrating strong inter-replicate consistency (r > 0.90). Differential expression analysis was conducted using Limma moderated t-tests with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction to identify temporally and strain-specific transcriptional programs. Multivariate ordination approaches including principal component analysis (PCA) and multidimensional scaling (MDS) with 95% confidence ellipses revealed progressive divergence of host transcriptional states according to both infection duration and viral pathogenicity. Functional enrichment analyses using Gene Ontology Biological Processes, KEGG, and Reactome pathways identified coordinated activation of antiviral signaling, inflammatory responses, antigen presentation, metabolic remodeling, and cellular stress programs. To further resolve systems-level immune reprogramming, predefined biological modules encompassing metabolism, ER/glycosylation/lysosomal pathways, MHC-I/II antigen presentation, and integrated stress response (ISR) signatures were quantified using Z-score–based module scoring. Temporal comparisons, particularly 24-hour versus 3-hour contrasts within each viral condition, demonstrated that highly pathogenic strains such as H5N1 and H7N9 induced stronger and more sustained inflammatory, stress-associated, and immune-regulatory transcriptional responses compared with seasonal H3N2, highlighting distinct pathogenic trajectories in airway epithelial antiviral immunity.
